Method

1

Make the binding paste

Boil the squash until tender, drain, and blend until smooth — add a splash of cooking water only if needed. Return to the pan, stir in the starch, and cook until thick. Cool completely.

2

Brine the cabbage

Dissolve the salt in cold water, add the cabbage, and mix well. Leave for 1 hour, turning once or twice, then rinse well and drain 15–20 minutes.

60:00
3

Mix the raw ingredients

Combine the cooled paste with all raw ingredients. If using gochugaru, let it rest so the flakes hydrate and deepen in colour.

25:00
4

Coat the cabbage

Add the drained cabbage to the mixture. Fold and massage gently until every piece is evenly coated.

5

Pack the jars

Pack firmly into sterilised jars, leaving about 2.5cm headspace. Press down to remove air pockets and keep everything submerged below the liquid.

6

Ferment at room temperature

Choose how long, then leave out until small bubbles appear and the aroma turns lightly tangy. Refrigerate once done — and start the tracker above when you jar it.

Notes

Fermentation guide. Days 1–2: fresh, crisp, lightly fermented. Days 3–14: the best eating window — balanced, tangy, still crunchy. After day 14: more sour and softer, with possible slight bitterness.

Storage. Refrigerate once fermented. Use a clean utensil each time and keep the vegetables submerged. Best within two weeks for crunch and freshness.

Serving. Steamed rice, tofu, bibimbap, noodles, fried rice, Korean pancakes or grilled vegetables.

Variations. Traditional — napa cabbage or daikon, more gochugaru. Spring greens — swap in pak choi, mustard greens or spinach. Orchard — add julienned pear and toasted sesame seeds for a sweeter, fruitier kimchi.
